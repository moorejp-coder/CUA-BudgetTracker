# Judge briefing script (read at 09:00)

Use this script verbatim so all three judges hear the same instructions.

**Script - Faculty Chair to Judges**

Thank you for judging the AI Vibe Coding Competition. Three things to know.

First, the rubric. Each project gets scored on six criteria, weighted from 10 to 25 percent. Functionality is worth the most at 25; problem identification and AI integration are next at 20 each. The full descriptions are in your scoring workbook on the Rubric_Reference tab.

Second, score during each Q&A, not during the demo. The student needs the room's attention while they're presenting. Use the demo itself to take notes - finalize the numbers in the three minutes after each project.

Third, hold your toughest questions for deliberation. Floor Q&A is three minutes only. If a project has a problem worth digging into, write it in the Notes column and we'll discuss after lunch.

The student is scored on what they built, not what they're naturally good at. A beginner who clearly stretched outranks an experienced student who coasted.

# Tie-break procedure

1. If two projects tie on average score: judges discuss them with a focus on the **AI Integration & Innovation** criterion (the spirit of the contest).
2. If still tied after discussion: faculty chair casts the deciding vote, with the tie-break reasoning recorded in the Notes column of the Final_Rankings sheet.
3. If three or more projects tie: the chair may award co-runner-up positions. The first place prize is awarded to exactly one project - split-prizes complicate sponsor logistics and travel.

# Judge-disagreement protocol

The Final_Rankings sheet flags any project with a judge **standard deviation above 10** in red. That is the threshold where one judge's view is materially different from another's. For each flagged project:

- Faculty chair asks each judge to state their one-sentence reasoning.
- Other judges may adjust their score (or not) after hearing the rationale. **No pressure** - disagreement is fine if it's principled.
- Document the substance of the disagreement in the Notes column. This is useful for next year's contest design.

# If something breaks

## A student's live URL is down at demo time

- Tech lead checks WiFi first. Then asks the student to refresh on their phone.
- If still down: student gets **2 extra minutes** at the end of the demo block to retry. They lose them if they can't recover.
- If unrecoverable: student presents from screenshots and their GitHub repo. **They are not disqualified**, but the Functionality score will reflect what was demonstrable.

## Projector or room display fails

- Tech lead switches to backup laptop (have one staged).
- If both fail: presentations move to crowd around the podium laptop. Less elegant, still works.

## WiFi fails for the whole room

- Tech lead activates hotspot from their phone for the podium laptop only. Judges score from the demo URL on the podium screen.
- If the room hotspot also fails: postpone by 30 minutes and provide AWS regional status check to confirm it's not on AWS's end.

## A judge cannot attend at the last minute

- Faculty chair reaches out to the backup judge **named on the contingency roster**. (Maintain a list of two backup judges agreed in advance - at least one faculty and one alum / industry contact.)
- If no backup is reachable: proceed with two judges. The Final_Rankings tab still calculates averages and std-dev correctly with two scores; just delete the third judge column from the average formula.
